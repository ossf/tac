# 2026 Q3 Vulnerability Disclosures WG

## Overview

**Mission**: The OpenSSF Vulnerability Disclosures Working Group seeks to help improve the overall security of the open source software ecosystem by helping mature and advocate well-managed vulnerability reporting and communication. We serve open source maintainers and developers, assist security researchers, and help downstream open source software consumers.

The Vulnerability Disclosures WG remains a [Graduated-level](https://github.com/ossf/tac/blob/main/process/working-group-lifecycle.md) Technical Initiative. Community health is stable. The bi-weekly AMER full-WG meeting and the APAC meeting both continue to be well attended, and the OSV-specific APAC-friendly session added last quarter is now a routine part of the cadence.

Building on [Q2](https://github.com/ossf/tac/blob/main/TI-reports/2026/2026-Q2-VD-WG.md), this quarter moved several long-running workstreams from discussion into concrete output:

- The AI-generated report quality workstream gained a **formal academic research partner**. A Georgia Tech SSLab team presented a user study on best practices for AI-generated vulnerability reports and is now running an IRB-approved interview program with maintainers, with the WG supplying report corpora and maintainer contacts.
- The **Ortelius sandbox sponsorship** question was worked through a structured scope/contribution/better-home/precedent evaluation and put to a WG vote.
- The **OSV schema shipped two releases** (v1.8.0 and v1.9.0), adding severity attribution, wildcard package support for end-of-life records, and the WordPress ecosystem.
- The vulnerability guides are now **published as GitHub Pages**, closing out a multi-quarter documentation-accessibility effort.

The WG also began an explicit conversation about **sustainability and the relationship between the WG and the projects it sponsors**, which is the most significant structural item for the TAC to be aware of.

**WG Project Board:** https://github.com/orgs/ossf/projects/29

## Activity #1 — AI-Generated Vulnerability Report Quality

https://github.com/ossf/wg-vulnerability-disclosures/issues/178

### Purpose

To develop best current practices for open source maintainers handling high-volume, AI-assisted vulnerability submissions, and to improve submission quality from finders. This quarter the workstream shifted from WG-authored guidance toward **evidence-based guidance grounded in academic research**, so that recommendations about report structure and triage are backed by measured data rather than anecdote.

### Current Status

- A research team from the **Georgia Tech SSLab** (advised by [Prof. Taesoo Kim](https://www.scs.gatech.edu/people/taesoo-kim)) presented their user study, *"Finding best practices for AI-generated vulnerability reports,"* to the full WG on July 8. Presenters included Youngjoon Kim, Mingyi Liu, and Andrew Chin.
- The study's framing matches the WG's own problem statement: false positives and hallucinations, over-claiming and context-blind reports, and — the team's primary focus — **developer-unfriendly reports** that are too long, insufficiently specific, and excessively deferential in tone.
- Method: review real reports alongside maintainer feedback (~600 Jira-sourced reports being triaged in collaboration with **OSTIF**), compare outputs from different models for the same bug, and measure which report fields actually affect triage time and accuracy in order to derive an optimized report template.
- The team is coordinating with **Bugcrowd** on triage-side input; Bugcrowd's stated concerns are scalability of pushing fixes and representation of business impact.
- As of August, the team has drafted its interview protocol, is submitting an **IRB proposal and completing CITI training** at Georgia Tech, holds a recurring feedback session with South Korean researchers, is collecting AI-generated reports from Berkeley, and is drafting an experiment dashboard.
- WG members offered concrete support: CRob offered access to a supply of AI-generated reports via **Akrites**, and Dave Welch reported a lead on procuring AI-generated reports across several major OSS projects within 30–60 days. Jeff Diecks is coordinating a follow-up session.
- The [AI-Slop Impact community survey](https://github.com/ossf/wg-vulnerability-disclosures/issues/181) has now closed.
- Ecosystem context discussed: Zig's no-AI content policy and libxml2's ban on LLM-generated content illustrate that some projects are responding with outright prohibition, which does not remove the underlying risk since attackers will still find the vulnerabilities.

### Discussion Themes Worth Recording

- **Provenance labels are not a free win.** Labeling reports as AI- vs. human-generated may be poorly received by maintainers if labels are inaccurate; maintainers react badly to feeling experimented on.
- **Public advisory databases are the wrong dataset.** Published records are post-curation and exclude invalid or rejected reports, which are exactly the examples needed. Access to *original, as-received* reports is the key blocker, and is gated on vendor confidentiality.
- **Prompt and output structure matter.** Requesting structured output (e.g., JSON with defined fields) produces more consistent, reviewable reports, and identical prompts across models are not necessarily a fair comparison.
- **Automated validation is not yet reliable.** An "adversary agent" step that attempts to reproduce a PoC before submission is promising but does not currently remove the need for manual verification.

### Up Next

- Georgia Tech expects to begin maintainer interviews once IRB approval lands; **the WG will help recruit maintainer participants and supply AI-generated report corpora**.
- Continue integrating findings into the WG's maintainer- and finder-facing guidance.
- Follow-up session being scheduled by OpenSSF staff to connect the research team with report-corpus sources.

## Activity #2 — Ortelius Sandbox Sponsorship Decision

https://github.com/ossf/wg-vulnerability-disclosures/issues/185

### Purpose

To decide whether the VDWG is the right sponsoring home for [Ortelius](https://ortelius.io/), which is seeking to move from the [Continuous Delivery Foundation](https://cd.foundation/) to OpenSSF. Ortelius focuses on post-deployment vulnerability remediation and tracking vulnerabilities on live systems.

### Current Status

- The WG ran a structured evaluation of the proposal on July 8, organized around four tests:
  - **Scope** — does post-deployment vulnerability awareness fall within a mission of promoting sound vulnerability *disclosure* practices, or is the WG's scope limited to the disclosure act itself?
  - **Contribution** — what would Ortelius concretely contribute back to disclosure workflows (e.g., generating VEX statements, feeding data back to OSV, identifying undisclosed vulnerabilities)?
  - **Better home** — would another venue (Supply Chain Integrity WG, a proposed SBOM/VEX-focused WG, or directly under the TAC) provide and receive more value?
  - **Precedent** — how does Ortelius compare to existing sub-projects (CVD Guides = disclosure guidance, OpenVEX = exploitability exchange, OSV = database/schema, SIREN = coordinated response)?
- A gap analysis contributed by Josh Dembling observed that the proposal is **heavy on consuming disclosure outputs** (CVEs, SBOMs, VEX) **and lighter on contributing back to disclosure processes**, with most of the value proposition sitting in remediation and operations.
- Ortelius's response: its deepest integration is with OSV.dev, consuming vulnerability intelligence to identify newly published vulnerabilities running on endpoints that pre-deployment SCA missed; it correlates SBOMs with CVE data post-deployment and integrates SPDX, CycloneDX, and VEX. It positions itself as a scaling layer for CLI-driven tooling — analogous in ambition to OpenSSF Scorecard — and explicitly does **not** want to be categorized as an SBOM tool.
- A WG vote was opened on issue #185, with WG leads deliberately voting last to avoid influencing the outcome. Final calls for votes went out in late July and again in mid-August.
- **As of this report, issue #185 remains open pending formal closure of the vote.**

### Up Next

- Formally close the vote and record the outcome on issue #185.
- If the WG sponsors the project: Ortelius submits a PR to the TAC repository using the project sandbox template, and a WG member is identified as champion/sponsor. Ortelius has confirmed its submission materials are prepared.
- If the WG declines: refer the project to a more suitable venue per the "better home" test.

### Questions/Issues for the TAC

**The WG is asking the TAC for guidance on where the boundary of "vulnerability disclosure" sits relative to adjacent initiatives, and ideally for durable criteria rather than a per-project ruling.**

**What surfaced it.** The Ortelius proposal asked the WG to sponsor a project focused on post-deployment vulnerability remediation — identifying newly published vulnerabilities running on live endpoints that pre-deployment SCA missed. Evaluating it, the WG could not answer a first-order question from its own charter: is our remit the *act* of disclosure (reporting, coordinating, publishing), or the *outcome* disclosure exists to serve (that affected software actually gets identified and fixed)?

**Why it is genuinely ambiguous.** The gap analysis on the proposal observed that the project is heavy on consuming disclosure outputs — CVEs, SBOMs, VEX, OSV.dev data — and lighter on contributing back to disclosure processes, with most of its value in remediation and operations. That is a fair reading. It is also true that the WG's existing portfolio is not purely about the act of disclosure: OSV is a database and schema, OpenVEX is an exploitability exchange, and SIREN is coordinated response. A consumption-heavy project is not obviously out of family.

**Why it will recur.** This is not a one-off. A proposal for a new SBOM/VEX-focused WG is anticipated, and the Supply Chain Integrity WG already holds adjacent ground. Without shared criteria, each new project in this space costs a WG a multi-meeting scoping debate, and similar projects may land in different homes depending on who evaluated them.

**What would help.** Guidance on the questions the TAC wants WGs to apply — for example, whether contribution back to a WG's core workflows should be a sponsorship requirement or merely a positive signal, and how much weight "no better home exists" should carry. The WG applied a four-part test (scope, contribution, better home, precedent) and would happily standardize on TAC-endorsed criteria instead.

**Framing note.** The WG treats project placement as a reversible decision — a project can start in one WG and move later — so this is a request for clarity that reduces recurring cost, not a blocker.

## Activity #3 — OSV Project (Schema and Database)

https://github.com/ossf/osv-schema

### Purpose

To maintain and advance the OSV schema and database so they meet current standards and community needs, and to complete the OSV schema's graduation to a Graduated Technical Initiative.

### Current Status

- **[v1.8.0](https://github.com/ossf/osv-schema/releases/tag/v1.8.0) released July 9, 2026**, adding attribution for CVSS strings. The severity-source field currently supports `self`, `CVE`, and `NVD`. The WG discussed whether to add further enums (e.g., GHSA, PySec) or allow pointing at any available source, and whether to express *verified* severity. The field's original intent was to serve downstream users who are required to accept a CVSS score from a specific data provider.
- **[v1.9.0](https://github.com/ossf/osv-schema/releases/tag/v1.9.0) released August 6, 2026**, adding wildcard package name support ([PR #560](https://github.com/ossf/osv-schema/pull/560)), the WordPress ecosystem, and updated guidance on CVE-prefixed records.
  - Wildcard support exists to represent **end-of-life** situations — "affecting everything moving forward" — so scanners can surface that the software itself is EOL rather than leaving users with a false sense of security.
  - The WordPress ecosystem work extracts plugin ecosystem data from CVE records, focused on Wordfence: roughly 10k CVEs identified, of which approximately 7k are already processable.
- Open schema design discussion: [issue #568](https://github.com/ossf/osv-schema/issues/568) proposes an ecosystem for AI agent "skills" (MCP / agent skill registries). Git hashes appear to be the most workable identifier, but coverage remains difficult and a registry-based approach is also under consideration. **Input is welcome.**
- **Graduation:** the [graduation application PR](https://github.com/ossf/tac/pull/456) remains open in the TAC repository. The team met during the quarter on security baseline and lifecycle documentation.
- OpenSSF is running an article series and the OSV team plans to contribute a piece on why the schema exists and how it is used.

### Up Next

- Resolve the severity-source enum question and the AI agent "skills" ecosystem proposal.
- Continue closing out the remaining security baseline and lifecycle documentation requirements. Security Baseline tracking milestone: https://github.com/ossf/osv-schema/milestone/1
- Publish the OSV schema article as part of the OpenSSF series.

## Activity #4 — Vulnerability Guides and Documentation Accessibility

https://github.com/ossf/wg-vulnerability-disclosures/issues/187
https://github.com/ossf/wg-vulnerability-disclosures/issues/188

### Purpose

To make the WG's vulnerability disclosure guidance easier to find, read, and consume by publishing it as a browsable site rather than as raw Markdown in a repository, and to expand guide coverage to audiences that are currently underserved.

### Current Status

- **[ossf/oss-vulnerability-guide PR #59](https://github.com/ossf/oss-vulnerability-guide/pull/59) was merged on August 19, 2026**, adding the templates, configuration, and content needed to publish the guide as GitHub Pages.
  - The original approach published from a `docs/` folder, which resulted in two copies of the guide's Markdown files. The merged approach publishes from the repository root instead, avoiding duplication. Changes were formatting-only; no guidance content was altered.
- The **CVD Guide for OSS Consumers** ([issue #115](https://github.com/ossf/wg-vulnerability-disclosures/issues/115)) is still **seeking contributors**. This has been an open ask for multiple quarters.
- Two topics were tabled for a future session and remain open:
  - **Academic researcher guidance** — following up on whether a sanitized version of Intel's internal document for academic researchers can be shared, and whether this should extend the existing guides or become a separate resource.
  - **CVD guide updates for evolving maintainer norms** — specifically, giving maintainers explicit permission to decline to fix something (and what to weigh in that decision), and guidance on when to acknowledge a report.

### Up Next

- Complete cleanup and process improvements for the GitHub Pages publishing flow (issue #188).
- Recruit contributors for the CVD Guide for Consumers.
- Resolve the tabled academic-researcher and maintainer-norms guidance questions.

### NIST RFI on Modernizing the NVD

The OpenSSF Public Policy Committee is seeking participants to help craft a response to NIST's Request for Information on modernizing the National Vulnerability Database in the age of AI. This is directly in the WG's subject area and is time-bound.

- NIST announcement: https://www.nist.gov/blogs/cybersecurity-insights/shaping-nvd-future-we-need-your-feedback-ai-enabled-vulnerability
- Federal Register notice (docket NIST-2026-0100): https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/08/12/2026-16371/request-for-information-rfi-on-modernizing-the-national-vulnerability-database-in-the-age-of
- Comment period closes October 13, 2026.

**The WG encourages the TAC and other TIs to contribute**, as NIST is explicitly asking about AI in the vulnerability management lifecycle, interoperability with disclosure programs and vendor advisories, and governance and data-quality controls — all areas where OpenSSF TIs have direct expertise.
