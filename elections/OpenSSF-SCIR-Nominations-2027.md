**Why do you want to serve on the OpenSSF TAC?**

After contributing to and serving as a lead maintainer of several OpenSSF technical initiatives, I decided to run for the SCIR seat last year with a clear goal: to help bring greater coordination and cohesion to our ecosystem, creating an environment where projects can grow, collaborate, and thrive.

We've made significant progress over the past year, but much of this work is still underway. I'm running for another term to help maintain that momentum and see these efforts through.

Some of the key developments we've been working on include:

- **Supply Chain Security (Priority 1):** We've drafted the proposal for the Governing Board's first priority, focusing on enabling the safe consumption of open source software by strengthening the security of its supply chain.
- **SBOM/VEX Working Group:** The new working group has been approved, and we're now beginning to put its vision and plans into practice.
- **Supply Chain Integrity Working Group:** We're reshaping the group around the principles of Priority 1, while ensuring that the needs of open source maintainers remain at the center of its work.

I believe 2027 will be the year when these efforts begin translating into tangible improvements in the security of open source projects and the broader ecosystem.

I'm running to continue helping shape that direction, strengthen coordination across initiatives, and turn our shared priorities into meaningful results. At the same time, I fully intend to remain hands-on: contributing code, advancing technical initiatives, and helping drive their adoption across open source.

For me, effective governance and technical work go hand in hand. I hope to continue contributing to both.
